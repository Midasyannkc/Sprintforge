Title: Boeing 737 MAX MCAS Crashes
Reported dates: October 29, 2018 (Lion Air Flight 610); March 10, 2019
(Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302, variant)

Description: A Boeing 737 MAX aircraft operated by Lion Air encountered a
catastrophic failure when faulty sensor data caused an automated maneuvering
system to repeatedly push the plane's nose downward. The plane crashed into
the Java Sea, killing all 189 people on board. The underlying cause involved
the Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System (MCAS). An erroneous
angle-of-attack (AOA) sensor provided incorrect data, triggering the
automated system to command repeated nose-down trim inputs. Despite pilots'
attempts to regain control, they were unable to overcome the system's
persistent downward pitch commands, ultimately leading to loss of control and
impact with the sea. A variant incident occurred when Ethiopian Airlines
Flight 302 crashed on March 10, 2019, under nearly identical circumstances
involving the same MCAS malfunction and faulty AOA sensor data.

Developer/Deployer: Boeing (developed and deployed the MCAS system)
Affected: Airplane passengers and crew members
