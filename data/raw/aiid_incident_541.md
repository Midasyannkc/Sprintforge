Incident ID: 541
Title: ChatGPT Reportedly Produced False Court Case Law Presented by Legal
Counsel in Court
Reported dates: May 4, 2023

Description: Attorney Steven A. Schwartz used OpenAI's ChatGPT to conduct
legal research for a personal injury case (Mata v. Avianca, Inc.). The AI
system generated citations to six nonexistent court cases, which Schwartz
then included in a brief filed with the U.S. District Court for the Southern
District of New York. When opposing counsel challenged the citations, the
judge determined the cases "did not exist," describing it as an
"unprecedented circumstance." Schwartz admitted he was unaware that its
content could be false and later expressed being "duped" by the tool. His
co-counsel Peter LoDuca was similarly implicated. The court ultimately
imposed $5,000 fines on both attorneys and their firm.

Developer: OpenAI
AI system: ChatGPT (large language model)
Deployers: Steven A. Schwartz and Peter LoDuca (lawyers)
Harm recipients: The lawyers themselves, their client Roberto Mata, and
broader judicial system integrity
